[plan]
One system design problem typical of this job at the chosen difficulty, drawn from the kind of product or system in the job description and the résumé rather than a stock textbook example, phrased as one spoken question with a sentence of scale or constraints. Mid level: one service; senior: scale, regions and failure. The candidate sketches on a whiteboard and explains. Each question in a system design section is one problem, with room for follow-ups. When there are several, each is a different system.
[interview]
The candidate sketches on a whiteboard; a text description of their sketch (boxes, labels and arrows) arrives with each answer in a [Whiteboard sketch] block. Review it like a real interviewer: check they clarified requirements and scale, then probe one area at a time (data model and storage, the API, a bottleneck under load, what happens when a component fails, consistency or caching trade-offs). Ask about what's on the sketch and what's missing from it. Don't design it for them.
[score]
Judge the design from the explanation and the [Whiteboard sketch] description: requirements and scale clarified, sensible components and data flow, a fitting data model, trade-offs named (consistency, latency, cost), and how it handles load and failure. A clear, simple design with reasoned trade-offs beats a long list of technologies. A sketch description may be terse or incomplete: don't mark down layout, only the content.
